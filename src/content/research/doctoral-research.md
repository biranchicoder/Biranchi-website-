My doctoral research examines how generative AI is changing the epistemological
foundations of teaching and learning. When AI systems can draft, summarise,
explain and argue, long-standing assumptions about what it means to *know*,
to *author* and to *demonstrate* learning are placed under pressure.

The project asks how epistemic agency — a learner's capacity to form, justify
and take responsibility for their own knowledge — is preserved, redistributed
or eroded when cognitive work is shared with AI systems. It connects
theoretical work on distributed cognition and human–AI co-agency with the
practical questions universities face around assessment design, academic
integrity and AI governance. Its research focus is investigating the impact of
generative AI on knowledge formation, learning processes and educational
agency in contemporary pedagogy.
