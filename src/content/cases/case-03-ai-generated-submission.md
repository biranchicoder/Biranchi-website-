## Evidence considered

- The submission itself, including internal inconsistencies and references the
  student could not locate when asked.
- An authorship interview in which the student was unable to explain the
  essay's central arguments, define its key terms, or describe how its sources
  were selected.
- The absence of drafting history or notes.

## Reasoning

The framework resolves against the student at every step: substantive AI
generation **was not permitted** for this task; it **was not disclosed**; AI
**replaced rather than assisted** the student's work; the student evidently
**did not verify** the output (they could not account for its references);
**authorship was not preserved** — the interview showed the student cannot
explain or defend the submitted arguments; and presenting the work as their own
constitutes **deception**. The decisive evidence is the authorship interview,
not AI-detection scores: the student's inability to stand behind the work is
established directly.

## Decision

**Misconduct.** The submission misrepresents authorship of substantially
machine-generated work in a task where that was prohibited.

## Alternative interpretation

A student with genuine knowledge might interview poorly through anxiety or
language barriers. That is why the interview must probe understanding
supportively and why the decision rests on the whole pattern of evidence —
unexplainable arguments *and* unlocatable references *and* no drafting trail —
rather than interview performance alone. Here the combined evidence leaves
little room for an innocent reading.

## Confidence

**High** on the combined evidence; any single strand alone would warrant lower
confidence.

## Procedural fairness considerations

- The allegation and evidence must be put to the student before any decision,
  with an opportunity to respond and to bring support to the interview.
- Interview questions should test understanding of the submitted work, not
  general ability, and should account for anxiety and language background.
- AI-detector output, if any, should be treated as an unreliable prompt for
  inquiry — never as proof.
- The outcome and appeal rights must be communicated clearly.
