> instructional writing. Adapt it to your institution's actual policies before
> operational use.

## Purpose

This guide helps you move from a suspicion or disclosure of generative-AI use
to a decision that is fair, consistent and explainable to the student.

## Before you begin

1. Locate the rules that actually apply: the institutional integrity policy,
   the unit outline and the task instructions. The most specific instruction
   the student was given governs the case.
2. Write down what the student was told was permitted. If you cannot state it
   in one sentence, treat ambiguity as a factor in the student's favour.

## The six questions

Work through these in order and record your answer to each:

1. **Was AI use permitted?** Quote the applicable instruction.
2. **Was it disclosed?** Check the declaration against what the evidence shows.
3. **Did AI assist or replace the student's work?** Identify where the
   substantive intellectual contribution came from.
4. **Did the student verify the output?** Look for evidence the student checked
   accuracy, references and reasoning.
5. **Was authorship preserved?** Can the student explain and defend the work?
6. **Was there deception?** Distinguish an incomplete declaration from an
   actively false account.

## Reaching a decision

- All six favourable → **acceptable use**.
- Permitted, authored, verified, but not declared → **disclosure problem**;
  respond to the declaration failure, not as contract cheating.
- AI replaced the work, or the student cannot account for it → potential
  **misconduct**; follow the formal procedure.
- The rules do not decide the case → record it as **unclear** and escalate the
  policy gap, not the student.

## What not to rely on

- AI-detection scores are not evidence of authorship. Treat them, at most, as a
  prompt to look at the work itself.
- Fluency, vocabulary or "sounding like AI" is not evidence.

## Talking to the student

Put the specific concern to the student, show them the rule involved, and let
them respond before forming a view. An authorship conversation should probe
understanding of the submitted work — supportively, and with allowance for
anxiety and language background.
