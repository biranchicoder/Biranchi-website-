## The four habits

### 1. Check the rules for *this* task

AI rules differ between units and between tasks in the same unit. Before using
any AI tool, find the task instructions and answer: *what exactly am I allowed
to use AI for here?* If you cannot find an answer, ask your unit coordinator —
before submitting, not after.

### 2. Stay the author

Use AI to help you think, not to think for you. A useful test: could you
explain and defend every part of your submission in a conversation, without
notes? If a paragraph contains an argument you could not reconstruct yourself,
it is not yet your work.

### 3. Verify everything

AI tools produce confident text that can be wrong: invented references,
misattributed ideas, plausible-sounding errors. Anything an AI contributed must
be checked against real sources before it goes into your work. If you cannot
verify it, cut it.

### 4. Disclose honestly

If your unit requires a declaration, describe what you actually did — briefly
and specifically: *"I used [tool] to [what you used it for]."* Honest
disclosure of permitted use is never punished worse than concealment.

## Quick self-check before you submit

- [ ] I know what the task allowed, and I stayed within it.
- [ ] I can explain every argument in this work.
- [ ] I verified every fact and reference an AI touched.
- [ ] My declaration says what I actually did.

## If you are unsure

Ask before you submit. A question to your coordinator costs nothing; an
integrity process costs a great deal.
